The paper's question

Can a fleet of AI agents and devices keep itself healthy as verifiable, append-only state—without depending on a central server, elections, or synchronized clocks?

Six small interactions turn the paper's architecture into observable tests. Follow them in order; each adds one idea and names the evidence that matters.

What this guide does

It gives each major claim a concrete action, an observable result, and a precise limit on what that result establishes.

How to use this path

Act, observe, then interpret.

Open each standalone proof, perform only the listed actions, and compare the result with “Watch for.” The demos are explanatory models; the paper remains the source of truth for the reported estate runs and limitations.

Begin with chain order

Six terms, in plain language

Vocabulary for the route

Estate
The whole collection of devices, agents, and services being kept in a known state.
Frame
One content-addressed record that names what happened and fingerprints the frame it follows.
Tile
A frame that asks one scoped question, such as “what is out of compliance?”
Exhaust
The findings left by a scan; they become the exact to-do list for the next step.
Lease
A bounded permission to lead. Others wait until it ends and their signed rank permits action.
Dimension
One branch or aspect of history that can grow independently and later be compared or merged.

Ordered evidence path

Six claims, one accumulating argument

First establish trustworthy order. Then watch the system observe, heal, change leaders, cross a privacy boundary, recover stranded work, and reconcile independent histories.

Expected total: about 8 minutes
01 Chain is the clock

Expected time: 60 seconds

Order survives a bad clock

Two devices write one shared story even though one reports the wrong time.

What you are looking at

Two simulated machines, their local clocks, and a frame list. Each frame contains the fingerprint of the frame before it.

Do this

  1. Run the guided sample.
  2. Switch to wall-clock sorting, then back to dependency order.
  3. Tamper with frame 1's predecessor link.

Watch for

Time sorting can place a later action before its predecessor. Dependency order remains intact until the deliberate link mutation turns chain verification red.

What this demonstrates

Dependency links—not claimed timestamps—establish the sequence, and recomputation detects a changed link. The page does not model signatures or transport.

02 Scan and healing

Expected time: 90 seconds

Ask everyone once; touch only what changed

A scan tile fans one question across the estate, and its exhaust becomes the next round's bounded work list.

What you are looking at

Five devices, a hash-linked evidence log, and separate meters for scan responses and repair devices touched.

Do this

  1. Run the guided loop.
  2. Introduce one drift, scan, and heal it.
  3. Run the wasteful full-sweep mutation.

Watch for

All five devices report, but repair touches only the exhaust targets. Two identical reports declare a stable floor; the full-sweep mutation turns repair scope red.

What this demonstrates

A fleet-wide baseline can drive follow-up proportional to implicated or locally changed devices, then stop at a stable floor. Silent changes still require a trustworthy local signal or another baseline scan.

03 Succession

Expected time: 75 seconds

Replace a failed leader without a vote

A signed roster and lease define who may act next before failure occurs.

What you are looking at

Three ranked machines, a logical beat counter, one active lease, and a SHA-256-linked control log. Rank 1 starts as leader.

Do this

  1. Leave rank 3's observation lag enabled.
  2. Run the guided scenario and inspect the claim beats.
  3. Inject the unsafe rank-3 claim.

Watch for

Rank 2 waits through expiry and grace, rank 3 becomes eligible even later, and rank 1 returns only at the interim boundary. The unsafe claim turns the semantic verifier red.

What this demonstrates

Precommitted rank plus staggered waiting can produce deterministic failover without election messages. The teaching model does not authenticate the roster or prove partition liveness.

04 Membrane

Expected time: 60 seconds

Learn from public chains without publishing private ones

Public data may cross inward only after verification; private estate frames have no outward path.

What you are looking at

Separate public and private frame stores, exact counts and hashes, and a derived private view that may incorporate accepted public facts.

Do this

  1. Verify and read public data inward.
  2. Attempt a private-to-public export.
  3. Inject a conflicting public frame and try the inward read again.

Watch for

Valid public knowledge enriches the private view. Outward attempts leave public bytes unchanged, and conflicting input is refused.

What this demonstrates

The modeled transfer gate can admit verified public data while rejecting private-frame export. It does not rule out separate side channels.

05 Deterministic reattach

Expected time: 75 seconds

Recover stranded work without model judgment

A stranded frame declares exactly what it read, so candidate histories can be checked by comparison rather than interpretation.

What you are looking at

One stranded frame, its declared read names and content addresses, and an ordered ladder of possible bases across local and public dimensions.

Do this

  1. Compare the stranded reads with each candidate.
  2. Run the fixed-order search and note the first accepted base.
  3. Tamper with one declared address and run it again.

Watch for

Contradicting bases are rejected exactly. The first contradiction-free base wins, provenance is recorded, and changing an address changes the result predictably.

What this demonstrates

Recovery can be a deterministic set comparison when declared reads and candidate bases are complete. No language model has to guess between the supplied histories.

06 Merge fidelity

Expected time: 75 seconds

Merge agreement; preserve disagreement

Two histories formed apart are joined by dimension and logical tick, with compatibility scored rather than assumed.

What you are looking at

Two independent histories containing an aligned shared dimension, a contradictory shared dimension, and data present on only one side.

Do this

  1. Run the deterministic comparison.
  2. Inspect the fidelity numerator and denominator.
  3. Enable the overwrite mutation and compare the assertions.

Watch for

Aligned and one-sided dimensions merge. Contradictions remain visible as parallel alternatives; the mutation turns preservation red.

What this demonstrates

Independent histories can combine useful agreement without hiding conflict or appointing an arbitrary winner. The equal-weight fidelity score is not a measure of business importance.

How to interpret failure

  • A red result after a labeled mutation or tamper action is expected. It demonstrates that the proof can reject a broken rule instead of painting everything green.
  • A red result on the normal path is evidence, not decoration. Reset once and repeat the listed steps. If it persists, treat that assertion as unproven rather than explaining it away.
  • A teaching page is not the reported field experiment. Use the matching proof-prompt link to inspect the exact claim, then return to the paper's reduction-to-practice and limitations sections for the empirical denominator.
